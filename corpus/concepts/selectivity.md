**Selectivity**, the third of IPI's four dimensions, asks whose signal you are actually seeing:
whether the binding you observe comes from the protein you meant to detect or from one of its
relatives. In practice it is established by measuring cross-reactivity against related family
members, the paralogs, isoforms, and splice variants that could account for the same signal.

**Selectivity** is a separate question from engagement, and evidence of engagement does not
establish selectivity. An antibody may bind its target with high affinity and bind close
paralogs just as well. Only a comparison across those related proteins distinguishes a selective
reagent from one that merely binds tightly.

The reason this matters practically is that a positive result in an application is rarely
self-interpreting. One band at the molecular weight predicted for the target, or staining in the
expected compartment, is consistent with the intended target but does not establish it. An
off-target protein can migrate to a similar position on a blot, and staining seen in a cell or
tissue may arise from the intended target, an unintended one, or both. **Selectivity** evidence
is what converts a plausible result into an attributable one.
