Rules
Gerunds, sentence case, one ellipsis, under four words where possible. Never name the corpus, retrieval, routing, tools, documents or the model. Never promise a finding the answer might not contain — "Gathering sources" is safe, "Found 3 studies" is not. Abstain and refuse replies skip the working beat entirely.

The serif isn't a style choice borrowed from Claude — it's the same argument the site already makes. proteininnovation.org sets its headlines and section ledes in a serif; the widget answering in one makes it feel written by the institute rather than bolted on by a vendor. Will people read it as an antibody?
Short answer: your researchers will, immediately — the upright Y is the universal way an antibody is drawn, in every textbook and every figure. Everyone else reads “Y”, which costs you nothing: a launcher only has to say “tappable, and this brand”. Nobody meets the mark cold either — it arrives beside “Chat with us”, on IPI’s own site, and the panel it opens says “IPI antibody assistant” one line down.
If you want it to read more molecular, refine — don’t rotate · shown at 88 px and 30 px
A · As built
Even strokes, even angles. Cleanest at 30 px, and the most letter-like of the three.
B · Hinged ★
A slight bend where the Fab arms meet the hinge, and a heavier Fc stem — the proportions of a real immunoglobulin. Stops being a letter without stopping being simple. My pick if you want the extra read.
C · Two-chain
Lighter arm tips imply the light chains — the textbook two-tone diagram. Unmistakably an antibody to a scientist, but the tonal split gets muddy at 30 px and won’t survive a single-colour favicon.
Why not sideways or diagonal
Sideways
Diagonal
Rotating is the one move that actively hurts. Upright is the convention that makes it legible as an antibody at all — tilt it and you lose the reference without gaining another: sideways reads as a pipe fitting or a rotated arrow, diagonal as a generic sparkle. It also breaks the optical centring inside the circle, and turns the breathing animation into a wobble.
The real answer is context, not geometry
A launcher mark almost never carries meaning alone, and it doesn’t need to. Yours arrives with a label reading “Chat with us”, on IPI’s own pages, opening a panel headed “Abbie · IPI antibody assistant”. Anyone who cares what the shape is finds out within one second of tapping it — and anyone who doesn’t still got a clear affordance.
Worth remembering the mark is also doing a second job: it’s the silhouette the cartoon Abbie is built from. Keeping it upright is what lets her face drop onto the same shape for LinkedIn and slides. Rotate the launcher and the two stop being the same character.
My recommendation: ship B · Hinged if the antibody read matters to your director, otherwise keep A. Either way stay upright, and let the label do the explaining.
D · Hinged + two-chain, and the size rule that makes it work
Combining them is right, with one condition. The hinge is durable — it survives any size and any single colour. The two-tone is fragile — it needs room, and it dies the moment the mark has to be one colour (favicon, monochrome print, an embossed sticker). So don't pick one: make the two-tone a large-size treatment of the same hinged geometry, and drop it below 48 px. One mark, two states, no compromise at either end.
Expressive · 48 px and up
Hinged arms, heavier Fc stem, light chains at 62 % — a legible immunoglobulin. Shown at 96 and 60 px: hero art, the greeting mark in the panel, slides, stickers, print.
Solid · below 48 px
Same geometry, one colour — shown at 44, 30 and 22 px. The launcher, the header avatar, the activity mark, favicons, anywhere it may be printed flat.
Why the threshold is 48 px
Below it the light-chain segment is roughly two pixels of a lighter red on red — it stops reading as a second chain and starts reading as an antialiasing error or a rendering bug. The hinge, by contrast, still reads at 22 px: you can see it in the activity mark above. That asymmetry is the whole argument for splitting them.
What this costs you
Two SVGs instead of one, differing by two opacity attributes — the paths are otherwise identical, so it is genuinely one mark and never drifts into two. Name them mark-expressive and mark-solid so nobody has to guess which one to reach for.
Recommendation: ship this. Every place a visitor actually meets Abbie in the product — launcher, avatar, activity — gets the solid hinged mark, which is what the site's quiet pages want anyway. The expressive version does the explaining wherever there's room to see it, and both are the same molecule.

Sources · what the research products actually do, and the fix for repeats
Two findings settle this. First: every citation-forward product — Perplexity, ChatGPT search, Claude — collapses to a count by default and expands to the full list on click; the explicit guidance is to cap visible items at three, because long lists push the composer off screen. Second: for the repeats problem, science already has a convention. Papers are referred to by first author + year, which is short like a journal name but unique like a title.

The important rule. Abstain, redirect and refuse must never show evidence language. Those replies contain no sources, so "Gathering sources…" would promise something the answer can't deliver — the one way a status line can actually lie. They get the neutral opener and nothing more.
