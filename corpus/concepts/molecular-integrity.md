**Molecular integrity**, the first of IPI's four dimensions, asks the plainest question in validation:
is the material in the tube actually the molecule it says it is? Everything else rests on the
answer — if the identity of the reagent is uncertain, nothing measured downstream can be
attributed to it with confidence.

At IPI, integrity means the antibody sequence is defined and verified by mass spectrometry, so
the reagent is established rather than assumed.

**Integrity** also covers purity and stability, assessed by size-exclusion chromatography (SEC).
SEC resolves the intact antibody from aggregated material, which is how IPI establishes that a
preparation is pure and free of aggregation. Purity belongs to integrity because a preparation
that is partly aggregate is not the single defined reagent the rest of the evidence assumes.

The third component is reproducibility: whether the antibody can be produced again and be the
same reagent. IPI's answer is recombinant format, since an antibody defined by its sequence can
be made again from that sequence. Lot-to-lot reproducibility sits inside integrity for the same
reason purity does, because everything measured later assumes a single defined reagent.

**Integrity** says nothing about whether the antibody binds its target or works in an assay. It
establishes only that the reagent is defined, pure, and reproducible.
