IPI treats genetic perturbation, independent antibodies, and expression correlation as neither
dimensions of validation nor separate locations in the evidence space. They are approaches that
strengthen confidence in how evidence generated somewhere else should be interpreted. This is
where the framework departs from validation strategies that classify evidence by the experimental
approach used rather than by the aspect of validation that approach informs.

The distinction is practical rather than semantic. A knockout control adds no dimension to
establish and no separate place for evidence to come from; what it adds is confidence in how a
result generated in a particular assay and system context should be read. An immunofluorescence
signal that disappears in a knockout line is the same signal it always was, and losing it on
perturbation is what strengthens the case for reading that signal as the target.

Matched comparisons of target-positive and perturbed samples are particularly information-rich
among these approaches, because run within one assay and system context they can strengthen
**engagement** and **selectivity** at once while clarifying what the readout itself means. They are
still not decisive on their own. An antibody may cross-react with a relative that the system
examined does not express, so the comparison looks clean while the cross-reactivity remains.
Perturbing a target can also change the surrounding biology, which complicates interpretation in
its own way.

Independent antibodies, and correlation between signal and independently measured expression,
work the same way: each addresses a specific uncertainty in the available evidence rather than
adding a new category of validation.

Reading these methods for the evidence they contribute, rather than as steps required of every
antibody, is what lets validation proceed adaptively: establish what the available evidence
already shows, identify what remains uncertain, then choose the approach that speaks to that gap.
